\section{Reproduction results}\label{sec:repro}

\subsection{Released checkpoints}\label{sec:released}

We evaluated \code{hrnet_fidip.pth} (DarkPose + FiDIP) and \code{mobile_fidip.pth} (Pose-MobileNet + FiDIP) under their released test settings: GT boxes, flip test and DARK post-processing.
Their predictions were then scored by \code{eval_coco.py}; \cref{tab:repro}, upper block, gives the results.

\paragraph{HRNet.} The released HRNet checkpoint reproduces the published numbers \emph{exactly}:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*]
  \item \res{rel.fidip.test.ap}~AP (published 93.6);
  \item AP$_{50}$/AP$_{75}$ \res{rel.fidip.test.ap50}/\res{rel.fidip.test.ap75} (published 98.5/98.5);
  \item AR \res{rel.fidip.test.ar} (published 94.6).
\end{itemize}
Upstream's own test script prints the same AP (\res{rel.fidip.test.ap.upstream}). Over all \res{xcheck.pairs} run/split pairs, our evaluator agrees with upstream's printout to within \res{xcheck.maxdiff}~AP.
The same predictions score \res{rel.fidip.test.ap.fixed}~AP under the \texttt{fixed} protocol, \res{rel.fidip.test.clean.ap}~AP on Test100-clean, and \res{rel.fidip.val.ap}~AP on Val400.

\paragraph{MobileNet.} The released Pose-MobileNet scores \res{rel.mobile.test.ap}~AP, against a published 79.2.

\paragraph{Val400 versus Test100.} Val400 is lower than Test100 for every model we evaluated on both (\cref{sec:thresholds}). Part of the gap comes from validate500's tighter boxes: on the same 100 images, swapping in validate500's boxes lowers the released model from \res{sens.box.v100.ap} to \res{sens.box.v500.ap}~AP.

\subsection{Retraining FiDIP and the domain-adaptation ablation}\label{sec:retrain}

\paragraph{Recipe.} We retrained with the released code and yaml plus command-line overrides (D5): \code{TRAIN.CHECKPOINT} (the COCO DarkPose weights), \code{TRAIN.LAMBDA} ($5{\cdot}10^{-4}$), \code{TRAIN.PRE_EPOCH} (10), \code{DATASET.TEST_SET} (\code{val400}, so the per-epoch selection never sees Test100) and \code{SEED}.
The classifier pre-stage runs for 10 epochs (as in \code{_custom.yaml}) on the shipped adult set; the best Val400 epoch is kept; the same recipe with $\lambda=0.0$ isolates domain adaptation; each arm has \res{rt.seeds} seeds.

\paragraph{FiDIP reproduces.} The FiDIP retrains score \resms{rt.fidip.test.ap}~AP on Test100 at the Val400-selected epoch, and \res{rt.fidip.test.ap.final.mean}\,{\footnotesize$\pm$\,\res{rt.fidip.test.ap.final.sd}} at the final epoch. On Val400 they score \resms{rt.fidip.val.ap}.
Paired with the released checkpoint on the same images, the difference is \res{rt.vsrel.delta}~AP, with a 95\% CI of [\res{rt.vsrel.ci.lo}, \res{rt.vsrel.ci.hi}]. Under \texttt{fixed} it is \res{rt.vsrel.fixed.delta} [\res{rt.vsrel.fixed.ci.lo}, \res{rt.vsrel.fixed.ci.hi}] (\cref{tab:boot}).
\textbf{The training pipeline therefore reproduces the published 93.6, under a stricter protocol that never selects on the test set.}

\paragraph{The domain-adaptation gain does not reproduce.} With $\lambda=0$ the same recipe scores \resms{rt.ft.test.ap}~AP on Test100, \res{rt.ft.test.ap.final.mean}\,{\footnotesize$\pm$\,\res{rt.ft.test.ap.final.sd}} at the final epoch, and \resms{rt.ft.val.ap} on Val400.
FiDIP minus fine-tuning is \res{rt.delta.test.ap}~AP, 95\% CI [\res{rt.delta.test.ci.lo}, \res{rt.delta.test.ci.hi}].
The paper's +0.9~AP from domain adaptation (92.7 to 93.6) is therefore \emph{not} reproduced. The adversarial term neither helps nor hurts measurably at $n=100$.

Our $\lambda=0$ runs still contain the classifier pre-stage, so we also ran the paper-faithful plain fine-tune without it (\code{PRE_EPOCH} 0, R021--R023, D21). This control (repro-3) was registered at 07:23 EST, after the R010--R015 Test100 results were known; it is a reproduction control and feeds no selection. It scores \resms{rt.ftnp.test.ap}~AP on Test100 and \resms{rt.ftnp.val.ap} on Val400.

\paragraph{Other reproduction attempts} (Val400, one seed; all in \cref{tab:attempts}).
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*]
  \item \textbf{$\lambda$ sweep.} For $\lambda\ge10^{-4}$, accuracy falls monotonically as $\lambda$ grows: $10^{-4}$ scores \res{rt.sweep.l1e4}, $2{\cdot}10^{-3}$ \res{rt.sweep.l2e3}, and $10^{-2}$ \res{rt.sweep.l1e2} (\res{rt.sweep.l1e2.final} by the last epoch), against \resms{rt.fidip.val.ap} at $5{\cdot}10^{-4}$.
  \item \textbf{The paper's recipe} (Adam $10^{-3}$, 100 epochs, batch 64; R019) peaks at \res{rt.paper.val.best} at epoch \res{rt.paper.bestepoch}, then overfits, ending at \res{rt.paper.val.final}. It explains no gap: the shipped yaml already reproduces 93.6.
  \item \textbf{The README-literal training labels} (R020, the MINI-RGBD labels on the synthetic images) score \res{rt.readme.val.ap}, which is \res{rt.readme.delta}~AP against the retrains with the official labels.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Training dynamics: over- and underfitting}\label{sec:curves}

Every run logs the training loss, the Val400 loss and Val400 AP per epoch (\cref{fig:curves}).
For the FiDIP retrains, the training loss keeps falling, while the Val400 loss flattens after about epoch \res{fit.valloss.flat}.
Val400 AP, however, still rises: the selected epochs are \res{rt.fidip.bestepoch} of \res{rt.epochs} for $\lambda=5{\cdot}10^{-4}$ and \res{rt.ft.bestepoch} for $\lambda=0$. There is thus no AP overfitting within the released 20-epoch schedule.

A longer schedule does not help either. The paper's 10$\times$ learning rate reaches the same Val400 level by epoch 10 and then overfits, as described above. A late LR decay looks like the better lever than more epochs.
The data invite overfitting in two ways: each of the 200 real images is seen about five times per epoch, and the balanced sampler repeats the same batch order every epoch. The released recipe counteracts this with a low constant LR and frozen early layers.
